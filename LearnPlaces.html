ׅ Banaue Rice Terraces

The Banaue Rice Terraces are a 2,000-year-old system of irrigated mountain steps carved into the Cordillera mountains of Ifugao, Philippines, by the ancestors of the indigenous Ifugao people using basic tools and manual labor.

ׅ Puerto Princesa, Palawan

Puerto Princesa is the capital and largest city of the province of Palawan in the Philippines, famously known as the "City in a Forest" for its strong commitment to eco-conservation and green urban planning. Spanning an area of 2,381.02 square kilometers, it serves as the region's main gateway, commercial hub, and the home of the world-famous Puerto Princesa Subterranean River National Park—a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the New 7 Wonders of Nature. As a highly urbanized independent city, it seamlessly blends a clean, orderly urban center with rich coastal traditions, seafood dining, and island-hopping destinations like Honda Bay.


ׅ Aklan, Boracay

Boracay is a world-renowned resort island located in the municipality of Malay, Aklan, within the Western Visayas region of the Philippines. Situated just off the northwestern tip of Panay Island, this compact, seven-kilometer-long destination is celebrated globally for its powdery white sand beaches—most notably the four-kilometer White Beach—as well as crystal-clear turquoise waters, vibrant sunsets, and lively water sports. Following a major six-month environmental rehabilitation in 2018, the island operates under strict sustainability guidelines that preserve its natural beauty while supporting a thriving mix of luxury resorts, beachfront dining, and tropical leisure activities.

ׅIntramuros

Intramuros is the historic, 0.67-square-kilometer walled area located within the modern city of Manila, Philippines. Built by the Spanish colonial government in the late 16th century along the southern bank of the Pasig River, its name literally translates from Latin to "within the walls." During the Spanish colonial period, it served as the political, military, and religious center of the Spanish East Indies, featuring massive stone fortifications, cobblestone streets, and grand baroque churches. Although heavily damaged during the Battle of Manila in World War II, key landmarks such as Fort Santiago, San Agustin Church (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), and the Manila Cathedral have been preserved or restored, making it one of the country's most important cultural and historical districts today.
